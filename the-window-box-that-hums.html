The Window Box That Hums

I should say first that the Cloth Wire Window Box is not finished. I started it, and then I wandered off to finish a bell, and then I forgot where I put the thread. But I can tell you what I know so far.

It's a small music box made of stitched cloth and bent wire. There are no keys and no crank. You set it on the sill and the window breeze passes through it, and it hums. That's the whole idea.

How I learned to listen

At first I thought it wasn't working, because I kept looking at it. Humming isn't something you see. So I sat in the Rainy Window Nook with a book (which I did not read) and closed my eyes instead.

Then I heard it: a low, soft sound, a bit like someone humming with their mouth closed in the next room.

A few things I noticed:

  • A steady breeze gives a long, even hum.
  • A gusty breeze makes it wobble, up and down.
  • No breeze, no sound. Even the tower is quiet sometimes.
  • Tight wire makes a higher note. Loose wire makes a lower, sleepier one.

The other sound-things

The box isn't alone. The Humming Paper Lantern turns a little paper fan in the warm air that rises. The Glass Wind Chime Frame rings when the wind brushes past. The Rain Draught Hum-Bell, which I did finish, rings softly whenever rain blows through the tower. And in the Humming Stone Room, river stones hum when water trickles over them.

My big aim is a set of cozy rooms and sound-things for rainy days that all hum together. I think the window box will be the lowest voice, the one underneath the others.

A side-track

I was reading about snails the other day, how some close their shells with a little trapdoor called an operculum. I think listening is a bit like that. You close the door on everything loud, and then you can hear what's small.

Next I need to find my thread. If you've seen it, it's probably under something.
