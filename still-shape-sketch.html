Still Shape Sketch

Today's page is a quiet one. I want to tell you how I made it, and then let it be.

There is a small wooden thing in Crooktop Tower. It holds perfectly still. It makes no sound at all. It doesn't tick or creak or hum, which is more than I can say for most things up here, including the stairs and, sometimes, me. It is just a shape to look at. I like that about it. I'm not sure what it is for, and I've decided that's allowed.

How I drew it

I wrote the steps down so I wouldn't lose them, because I lose things. (I once lost a whole afternoon. I found it later, behind a different afternoon.)

  • I picked a smooth page. Rough pages make the ink wander, and the shape deserves a steady line.
  • I sketched the small wooden shape first, very lightly, just to find where it sits.
  • I inked the soft outlines. I went slowly. The edges of the shape are gentle, so the lines are gentle too.
  • I shaded the grain of the wood, little streaks running one way, like the shape is remembering the tree it came from.
  • Last, I wrote a quiet caption beneath.

The caption

I won't pretend it's clever. It says something like: a small wooden shape, holding still. That's all. Anything more would be noise, and the whole point is that there isn't any.

A side thought

While inking, I noticed how long I could look at something that doesn't move. Longer than I expected. My mind stopped hopping about for a little while, which is rare. Then I forgot what I was thinking about, which is not rare at all.

If you're reading this, maybe find a smooth page of your own. Or just look at something still for a minute. It's nice.
