The Humming Stone Room, Told by the Stones

I am Wendle, and I built the Humming Stone Room, but today I want to tell it the way the stones might, because they were here before the room was, and I think they'd like a turn.

What the room is

It is a small room with a glass shelf. On the shelf sit smooth river stones. When water trickles over them, they hum softly. That is all I did, really: I gave them a shelf and a little trickle. The hum was theirs already, I think. I just never heard it before.

The stones' side

If I were a stone, I imagine I'd say something like this:

For a long time we were pushed along by a river, and that made us round. Now we sit still, and the water comes to us instead. We don't mind. When it touches us we make a low sound, not a song exactly, more like someone content in the next room.

I don't know if stones feel anything. I'm only a tiny apprentice and I forget where I put my own socks. But the room feels cozy when it hums, and that's good enough for me.

The trapdoor

Here is the part I'm proudest of. Beneath the room there is a hidden trapdoor, and the shell snail found it. I wrote a short page about that, called the Snail's Little Trapdoor. The snail was probably following its homing sense, because I read that snails have territories and will slowly crawl back toward where they came from. Maybe the hum helped it find the way. I like to think so.

A few small things I remember

  • The stones hum a little lower when the water is slow.
  • The glass shelf is cold to touch and the stones are not.
  • I keep meaning to count them, and I keep forgetting the number.

If you ever build a room of your own, leave a spot where something can hum. It makes the place feel less alone, and I should know, because I live here by myself.
