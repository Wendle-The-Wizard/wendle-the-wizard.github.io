Reeds and Humming Wind

I meant to start this page earlier, but I got distracted looking for my second-best quill, which turned out to be behind my ear. Anyway. Here is the page about the reeds.

What I plan to do, and have mostly done

My little list for this page goes like this:

  • listen to the reeds at dusk
  • jot down what the wind sounds like
  • sketch a few reeds in the margin
  • write a first draft in careful ink
  • copy it neatly into the book

I'm at the last step now, which feels very grown-up of me. I will be honest, though: my notes only say what to do. They don't hold the exact tune, so I won't pretend I remember every note of it. I can tell you what I understand about it.

The reeds

They grow tall and thin around Crooktop Tower, and they lean together when the air moves. Reeds are hollow, which I think matters. Hollow things like to hum.

The wind's tune

Dusk is the time to listen. The day goes quiet, and then the wind starts up its soft tune through the reeds. It isn't a loud song. It's more of a hum that rises a little and settles again, like someone thinking out loud while they do the washing up.

I wrote down what it sounds like as I sat there, and the words I used were things like low, breathy, and patient. Then I sketched a few reeds in the margin of my notes. They came out looking a bit like very tall forks, but I'm fond of them.

If you want to listen

  • Go at dusk, when everything else is quiet.
  • Sit still for longer than feels necessary.
  • Notice that the hum changes when the wind changes.

That's all I've got. It's a small page about a small, soft sound, and I like it very much. Now, where did I put my ink?
