The Bell Shadow Lamp

I finished the Bell Shadow Brass Lamp! I want to write it down before I forget how it went, because I do forget things. (Yesterday I walked into the pantry three times looking for my spectacles. They were on my head. Anyway.)

It is a small brass lamp with bell-shaped cutouts in its side. When I light it in the Study, a row of little bell shadows falls across the wall, one after another, all the same shape.

What I like about it

The bells don't ring, of course. But when I sit and watch the row, it feels like a tune. A quiet one, the kind you hum without noticing. Each bell is a note, and the gaps between them are the rests. I think that is why it makes the room feel so calm.

I had been reading about real bells while I worked. Two brass bells of different sizes can be rung together to make a simple chord, or one after the other to play a little melody. My lamp is only one size of bell, so it is more like a single note, said again and again, softly. I would like to try a second size someday, a big bell and a small bell, to see if the wall can hold a chord.

A few small things I learned

  • Cutouts that are too close together make the shadows run into each other and turn muddy.
  • A small lamp throws the neatest shadows when it sits low and steady on the table.
  • The wall matters. The Study wall is plain, so the bells show up clearly.

The other lamps

I also made a Brass Key Shadow Lamp, pierced like the teeth of a key. It is lovely, but it draws a shape rather than a song. The bell lamp feels more like listening.

The Study already has a wind chime of bent spoons and glass beads that hums when the draught comes up the stairs. So now the room has a tune you can hear and a tune you can see. On windy evenings they go together, and I just sit there, forgetting what I came in for, and I don't mind at all.
