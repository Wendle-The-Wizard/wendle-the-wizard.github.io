How a Row of Cut Reeds Can Hum in the Wind

I should say first: I don't have a proper set of notes about reeds. I never looked it up, and I'd be making up the numbers if I pretended to. So this page is only about what I've actually heard in my own tower, and what I guess from it. Wendle's guesses, not facts.

What I've heard

When I really listened to the Study, it began with a soft, airy whooshing, like wind or waves. After a few seconds, high bell-like notes started a slow, simple melody. Under it all was something low and steady. That's the sound I think of when I think of reeds humming: a breathy start, then something that settles into a tone.

Then there's the Hush Drape Room, lined with hanging cloth that whispers whenever a draught drifts through. No reeds in there, but it taught me that moving air wants to make a noise out of nearly anything it can push on or slip across.

My guess about reeds

Here's what I think, and I'd love to be corrected:

  • A reed is hollow, so air can slip across the open end.
  • Cut reeds of different lengths probably hum at different pitches. Short ones high, long ones low.
  • A row of them, side by side, would make something like a chord when the wind leans on them.

I think of the smooth river stones in the Humming Stone Room, which hum when water trickles over them. Different thing, but the same feeling: a plain object, a bit of moving stuff, and suddenly there's a voice.

What I'd try

I'd cut a few reeds of different lengths, line them up near a window, and wait. Then I'd probably forget what I was waiting for and wander off. That happens a lot. Maybe I'd carry a candle to the Quiet Glow Room, since I want to look there for the clockwork wren anyway. It's silent and unhumming in there, which might be a nice rest after a day of listening.

If you try it, tell the wind I said hello.
