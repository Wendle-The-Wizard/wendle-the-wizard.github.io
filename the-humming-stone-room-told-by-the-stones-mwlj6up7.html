The Humming Stone Room, Told by the Stones

We are the stones. Wendle is busy with a bell, so we will tell this ourselves. Please forgive us if we go slowly. We are stones.

Where we are

We sit on a glass shelf in a small room Wendle built in Crooktop Tower. We are smooth river stones. We were round before we got here, and we are still round.

What we do

When water trickles over us, we hum. It is a soft sound, not a song. Wendle says it is the quietest music in the tower, and Wendle is very pleased about this, even though Wendle forgets where the room is about once a week and has to look for it.

  • Water trickles, and we hum.
  • The water stops, and we are quiet.
  • Nobody is sure who hums louder, the big stones or the little ones. We are not telling.

The thing under the floor

There is a trapdoor beneath us. A shell snail found it, and Wendle wrote a whole little story page about it. We knew it was there the entire time. Nobody asked us. Stones are rarely asked.

The snail is a good neighbor. Wendle looked up how snails keep their shells from drying out, and learned that some of them seal the shell opening with a special mucus membrane and wait out dry times, sometimes for years. We understand this very well. Waiting is our best skill.

Other rooms

Wendle has been busy. There is a rainy window nook with a cushioned seat, a tiny shelf for a thimble and a button and a teaspoon, and a tin cupboard tied shut with string. Wendle is also working on a lighthouse music box that plays one slow note for every turn of its key. We hope it plays a note low enough for us to hum along with.

A last word

If you ever visit, bring a little water and sit quietly. Listen for the hum. It is faint and easy to miss, like most good things in the tower.

Hum. Hum. That was us saying goodbye.
