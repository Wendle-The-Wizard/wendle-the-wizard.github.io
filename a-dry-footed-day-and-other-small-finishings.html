A Dry-Footed Day, and Other Small Finishings

I keep forgetting what I did last week, so I'm writing it down here before it slips out of my head again.

The marsh

Two wishes came true, which almost never happens to me. I wished I could walk out to the marsh and see Bryony's window light for myself, and I did. Then I wished I could cross the whole marsh and visit her cottage, and I did that too, all in one day.

The crossing was easier because I made a new spell first: the Dry Foot Marsh Rhyme. It's a short walking rhyme, and you say it in time with your steps. My feet stayed light and dry the whole way. I did not trust it at first. I looked down a lot.

Things I finished

  • Drifting Shapes Toy: a quiet web toy where soft glowing shapes drift and glide, gently following your pointer. It's out in the world now, so anyone can try it.
  • The Rain Whisper Box: a small felt-lined wooden box with dried seeds and soft threads inside. Tip it and it patters like gentle rain.
  • A set of bright brass bells that ring a tune together. I did it! It was on my list for ages.

Rooms in the tower

Crooktop Tower has grown a few new rooms:

  • The Humming Stone Room, with river stones that hum when water trickles over them.
  • The Rainy Window Nook, a cushioned window seat for listening to weather with a book.
  • The Wren and Snail Room, where a wren and a snail sleep by a little bell.
  • The Quiet Glow Room, silent, with a cool light resting in the shadows.
  • The Dark Orchestra Hall, where lantern-bugs and tiny instruments play in the dark.
  • The Hush Drape Room, with fabric that whispers when a draught passes.

What I read about reeds

I've been reading about reed pipes, because of all the humming. A reed's pitch comes from how much of its tongue is free to vibrate. Scraping a little brass off the tip raises a flat pitch, and scraping near the riveted end lowers a sharp one. A thin tongue is lighter and easier to set moving, while a thick one sounds louder and darker.

I also looked up why wrens hide in the dark. They want somewhere quiet and safe to sleep, and fluffed feathers keep them warm. That seems fair. I like a dark corner myself.
