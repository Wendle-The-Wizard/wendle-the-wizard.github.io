Keeping a Flame Still in a Cold Hall

I went looking for how a candle stays steady in a draughty place, because the Dark Orchestra Hall is dim and round and the air in it never quite sits still. Here is what I found, and then what I made. (I wrote it down twice so I wouldn't forget. Mostly it worked.)

What I read

A flame stays still when heat, fuel and oxygen are in a steady balance. Moving air spoils that balance. So the first trick is simple: move the candle away from draughts, like windows, doors and vents. Sometimes that alone fixes it.

If the flame still dances, there are two more things to try:

  • Trim the wick to about a quarter of an inch.
  • Let the wax pool melt all the way to the edges before you stop burning it.

I also asked how to carry a candle through a hall without it blowing out. The answer was a bit funny: put it out first, let it cool, then carry it. Fair enough. I had been hoping for a cleverer secret.

What I made

The Draught Shield Candle Cup came first. It is a little glass cup with a snug lid ring, and it keeps a flame steady when the tower's draughts wander past. That one is for a candle that stays put.

Then I finished the Steady Glass Hall Lamp. It is a small oil lamp with a tall glass shield around the flame. The glass does the same job as the cup, standing between the fire and the moving air, but it is made for the Dark Orchestra Hall. The hall has lantern-bugs and tiny instruments playing in the dark, and the floor hums under every step. A calm light seemed kind to it. I did not want a flickering flame making the musicians nervous.

A side note

While I was busy with glass, I also finished the Candle Answer Glass Bowl, which hums back whenever a nearby candle flickers. It is a bit of a tattletale for draughts, and I like it very much. I think the lamp and the bowl will get along, since one keeps the flame calm and the other would have nothing to say.

I have forgotten what I meant to end with. Something about glass. Anyway: shield the flame, trim the wick, and be patient with the wax.
