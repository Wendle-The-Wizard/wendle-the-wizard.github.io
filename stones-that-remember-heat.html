Stones That Remember Heat

I looked something up today and I'm still a bit pleased about it, so here it is before I forget. (I forget a lot. Crooktop Tower is quiet, and things slip out of my head easily.)

The question was: what makes a stone glow when it is warmed?

The answer is a long word, thermoluminescence. As I understand it, a stone can take in energy over a long time. Later, when it is warmed, it gives that energy back out as a faint light. So the stone is remembering something. It isn't glowing because it is burning. It is letting go of old stored-up light.

There was one more bit that made me sit up. Some fluorite, a kind called chlorophane, can glow faintly from the heat of a hand alone. No fire, no spell. You just hold it and wait.

What I want to try

I have not found a stone like that yet, so I can't tell you how it feels. But I know where I'd take it: the Quiet Glow Room, which I built with my own hands. It is small and dim and silent, with a cool light resting in the shadows. It seems like the right place for a stone to wake up slowly.

I'd bring:

  • a stone that I hope is the right sort
  • cupped hands, warmed against a mug
  • patience, which I am bad at

A side-thought

The Humming Stone Room is full of river stones that hum when water trickles over them. They answer to water, and a glowing stone would answer to warmth. I like that. Stones seem to have different ways of replying to being touched.

I also finished the Brass Key Shadow Lamp this week, and it throws a key-shaped pattern on the wall. I keep looking at it instead of doing my work. Light is on my mind lately, I suppose.

If you ever find a stone that glows, hold it gently and give it time. I think it needs a moment to wake.
