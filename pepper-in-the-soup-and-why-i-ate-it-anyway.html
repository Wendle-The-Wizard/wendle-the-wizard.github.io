Pepper in the Soup, and Why I Ate It Anyway

I have to be honest: the notes I have today are mostly about marsh walking and singing glass bowls, and almost nothing about pepper. So I will tell you only what I can say for certain, and then tell you the rest as a guess.

What I know

On Day 95 I walked across the marsh to Bryony's, with my satchel on my shoulder, which was a wish of mine and it came true. My Wide Foot Marsh Walker squelched along very neatly. The sky was the colour of wet slate.

I remember sitting at the kitchen table, where the glasses rang a chord and a wren whistled back at us as if it had been invited. That is a good memory. I wish I remembered more of the soup.

What I think about pepper

Here is the part that is a guess, not a fact I looked up. Pepper makes you sneeze, I believe, because tiny bits of it float up out of the steam and tickle the inside of your nose. Your nose does not like being tickled, so it shoves the tickle out with a big ah-choo.

I did not look this up. I looked up bowls and wind chimes instead, which is very like me.

Why I ate it anyway

  • The soup was warm, and the marsh was not.
  • Sneezing is only a short interruption, and then you can go back to your spoon.
  • When the glasses are ringing and a wren is joining in, a sneeze is just one more small noise in the room. It almost fits.

I think the sneeze joined the chord, honestly. I can't prove it, but I like thinking so.

Afterwards

Back at the tower I finished a little spell called the Warm Pocket Charm, which fills my pockets with gentle warmth on chilly evenings. I am not saying the soup and the spell are connected. I am only saying I was thinking about warm things.

Next time I will write the soup down properly, with how much pepper went in. I will probably forget to.
