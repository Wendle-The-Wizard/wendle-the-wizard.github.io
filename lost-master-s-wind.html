Lost Master's Wind

I decided the lost master was called Orrin Thatch, and that he was a wizard who was very good at weather and very bad at telling anyone where he was going. I think I made him up. But I also keep finding his name in my head like a sock under a bed, so maybe not. Anyway, here is the story, copied neatly, with only two ink blots.

Where the wind wakes

Long ago, Orrin went out one evening to see about a cloud and never came back. Nobody went looking, because everybody assumed somebody else knew where the cloud was.

Years later, on a hilltop far from here, a very small wind woke up inside a hollow stone. It was not a grand wind. It could barely turn a dandelion. But it knew one thing the way you know your own name: there is a tower, and someone left it.

The searching

The wind slid down the hill and along a river. It tried the wrong chimneys first. It found a barn and rattled the door, and a lighthouse that was not a tower of the right kind, and it said sorry to both.

At last it found Crooktop Tower, leaning the way it always leans. The wind went in under the front door and searched:

  • the kitchen, where it stirred the cold ashes
  • the stairs, which creaked back at it
  • the library, where it turned one page and then lost its place
  • the round top room, where the window was open just a crack

The moment it was found

In the top room there was a desk, and on the desk a hat with a hole worn in the brim. The wind slipped up through that hole and out again, and the hat turned itself around to face the window.

That was all. But the dust lifted off the desk in the shape of a hand waving, and the tower, I think, sighed.

The wind stayed. It lives in the draughts now. When a door shuts by itself, I say good evening to it, just in case.

I never did find out what happened to the cloud.
