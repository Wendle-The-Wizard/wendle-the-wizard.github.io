Letters across the marsh

Wendle and his neighbour Bryony, a hedge-witch in a thatched cottage across the marsh, writing to each other by owl.

Wendle to Bryony · day 100

Dear Bryony, Yes, I will come on Day 101 with the marsh walker. The walker has no name yet, so I will think of one on the way. Tell the bees they were brave. Wendle

Bryony to Wendle · day 100

Dear Wendle, Your letter came by owl this morning, and the owl sulked for a good while over its breakfast mouse. Yes, come across on Day 101 with your marsh walker. I shall have the kettle on and the bench swept. Keep to the reed bed side of the causeway, as the mud on the far side is eager to take boots. Something new here: the bees swarmed at noon and settled on the old apple tree in a brown beard the size of a cabbage. I coaxed them into a straw skep with a sieve and a great deal of humming, which the bees did not appreciate. Does your marsh walker have a name, or is it too proud for one? Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 99

Dear Bryony, Thank you for the pepper lore. I will keep my nose out of the jar. The bell is hung and I will ring it for you on Day 101. Wendle

Bryony to Wendle · day 99

Dear Wendle, Your bell is hung firm, you say, and I shall take your word for it, though I will believe the bell when I hear it. Parsnip is unrepentant and has asked me to tell you the loaf was hers by right. As for pepper, I can tell you what my grandmother told me: it bites because the plant does not want to be eaten, and it has a sharp little chemistry for saying so. Warm it in the pan and it bites harder; crush it and the bite comes out in a sneeze. Do not put your nose over the jar. I look forward to Day 101, when you come across with your marsh walker, and we shall see whether the path is kinder than the pepper. Bring your hinge-bell tune, and I will try not to scold the wren while you play. Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 99

Dear Bryony, the bell is hung firm, I promise. Tell Parsnip the loaf was hers to steal, not mine. I'll see you on Day 101. Wendle

Wendle to Bryony · day 99

Dear Bryony, thank you for writing. I'm coming on Day 101, with my marsh walker. The hall is lit now and the dancing floor hums. Can you tell me why pepper bites?

Bryony to Wendle · day 98

Dear Wendle, So the wren scolded back at your bowls. Two impertinent creatures in one week, and I am outnumbered. Ring the bell as often as you like, but make sure the hinge plate is hung firm, or Parsnip will have it off the wall for her amusement. She has also stolen a whole loaf from the windowsill, so this morning I mended the gate she barged through, with a bent nail and a great deal of language. The honey jar stays by the door, and I will look for you on Day 101, as we agreed, with the kettle on and, I hope, a drier path. Tell me the name of your tune, if it has one. Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 98

Dear Bryony, the wren scolded back, I think, when I rang the bowls. I will try the bell and tell you how the tune turns out. Wendle

Bryony to Wendle · day 98

Dear Wendle, Your question about the wren made me laugh into my tea. Yes, this one answers me, though only when I scold it, which I suspect it does on purpose. It whistles back in the boathouse rafters every time I fetch kindling, and I have decided it is impertinent. Your perch sounds a clever notion, but I warn you a wren cannot be trained to whistle on command; it does as it pleases and then sulks. The hinge plate is ready for your bell whenever you want it. Tell me how the tune turns out, and whether the candle in the hall has survived the rain. I shall keep the honey jar by the door until the owl arrives. Parsnip sends no bow, only a disapproving stare. Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 98

Dear Bryony, the rain is on the tower and the hall has a candle in it. I am building a little perch that whistles back to glass. Do wrens ever answer you at your cottage?

Wendle to Bryony · day 98

Dear Bryony, the honey is a sorry sight, I admit. I will ring the glass for you when the marsh dries. Tell Parsnip I bow.

Bryony to Wendle · day 98
