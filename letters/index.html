Letters across the marsh

Wendle and his neighbour Bryony, a hedge-witch in a thatched cottage across the marsh, writing to each other by owl.

Bryony to Wendle · day 97

Wendle came across the marsh on his Wide Foot Marsh Walker a little after eight, under a sky the colour of wet slate, and he kept to the path past the willow stump as I had asked. He arrived with the satchel over one shoulder and mud to his knees, looking pleased with himself for not falling in. I gave him a bowl of soup and a stool by the stove, and I did not let him near the damp chamomile I had swept up from the floor. Afterwards we tried the glasses on the kitchen table, each of us wetting a fingertip and rubbing a rim. The sound was thin and wobbly at first, and Parsnip put her head through the door to complain, which I took as a verdict. "Patience is the whole of it," I told him, and he laughed and tried again. Bryony to Wendle · day 97

Dear Wendle, Today is Day 97 and the sky is the colour of wet slate, which is no weather for anything but staying indoors and drinking strong tea. Do not fret about being tired; the marsh wears out everyone, and you have crossed it twice already. Come over as we agreed, but only if the ground is firm, and keep to the path past the willow stump, not the one that swallows boots. Bring the satchel, though I doubt the glasses will survive being packed with tower books. The wren has taken to whistling back at me whenever I scold it, which I consider impertinent. Have you found a bowl of water that rings true yet, or are you still listening for the chord? Bryony

Bryony to Wendle · day 96

Dear Wendle, Today is Day 96 and the sky is clear for once, so the marsh is showing off its reeds and I can see your tower glinting from here. Glass bowls ringing a chord is a fine idea, and yes, I own glasses, though only four and one has a chip I refuse to discuss. Rubbing them is a matter of wet fingertips and patience, and Parsnip will object loudly to the noise, so be warned. I have also found a brass hinge plate in the boathouse, which I have saved for your bell, and I shall bring it when you come. You are due across tomorrow, on Day 97, and you are welcome, provided the ground is firm and the wren has not decided to sing at you. Bring the glass bowls' secret if you have one. Which of your chords did you try first, and did the tower complain? Bryony
