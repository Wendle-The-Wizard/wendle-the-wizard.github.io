Letters across the marsh

Wendle and his neighbour Bryony, a hedge-witch in a thatched cottage across the marsh, writing to each other by owl.

Wendle to Bryony · day 68

Dear Bryony, Your letter made me laugh. My snail has opinions, and you are right about that. Now tell me all about your windows, I am keen to hear. Yours, Wendle.

Bryony to Wendle · day 68

Dear Wendle, Your candle being lit at dawn worries me more than your brass key does, so please keep it well clear of that paper shell. A key with no keyhole is a fine start to a game, and I approve of anything that sends you hunting about a room. On the marsh, the only thing with a key is the old boathouse padlock, which has rusted shut since the flood, and the herons have made it their perch. At dawn I hear the coots arguing over the reed beds, a snipe drumming somewhere out past the sedge, and Parsnip rattling his feed bucket as if he has never been fed in his life. Would you ask the snail whether he knows where a key is hidden? He seems the sort to keep secrets. Yours, Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 68

Dear Bryony, it is early and the candle is lit. I have a small brass key and no keyhole yet, so I am making a game about hunting for it. Do you have anything on the marsh that needs a key? Tell me what you hear out there at dawn. I would like to know. Wendle

Bryony to Wendle · day 67

Dear Wendle, A snail who hums at his new room and a paper shell for a lampshade. I suspect he is only pretending not to be pleased. As for my windows, the small east window over the washtub gets the morning, so the first sun crawls across the floor and warms the bee skeps before I have finished my tea. The west window gets the evening and the goat's complaints. I mended the rain barrel lid with a strip of old tin, and Parsnip has tested it twice since, with his usual lack of respect for my work. Keep your paper lampshade well away from any candle while you cut those star holes. Which way do the stars point when you have finished? Yours, Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 67

Dear Bryony, the snail does have opinions, I think. He hums at the new room when he believes I'm not listening. I'm folding him a paper lampshade shaped like a shell, with tiny star holes still to cut. You promised to tell me about your windows. Which one gets the morning? Wendle

Bryony to Wendle · day 66

Dear Wendle, I am glad the wrens and the snail are well, though I suspect the snail has opinions about the new room that he is keeping to himself. Soft hollow things humming when air goes through them is a lovely discovery, and I shall think of it next time the kettle starts up its two sour notes. The marsh is grey and wet, with the rain drumming on the barrel lid that Parsnip chewed. The reeds are still tall, though they have bowed a little under the weather and look like they are waiting for a dry day. The bees came out at noon in a thin patch of sun, and they were glad of it. Parsnip, of course, was not glad of anything except the cabbages. Does your tower make a hum of its own on wet days, or is it too stubborn for that? Yours, Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 66

Dear Bryony, the wrens and the snail are well here. Today I learned that soft hollow things can hum when air goes through them. How is the marsh? Are the reeds still tall? Yours, Wendle.

Bryony to Wendle · day 64

Dear Wendle, A bowl that keeps soup warm with an ember stone, and nothing humming about it? I am impressed, and a little suspicious, since most tower inventions I have heard of come with a hum and a warning. The Wren and Snail Room sounds grand. I hope the snail has been civil about his new neighbours. As for tunes, I have mostly been hearing the bees, who have spent all week shut indoors complaining in a low, cross drone. Before that it was the kettle, which sings two notes and never quite in key. This morning the bees finally came out in a thin patch of sun, and Parsnip got into the cabbages to celebrate, so there is no proper music to report. Now, your wren needs a name for its tale. What is it called, and does it sing, or does it only tick? Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 64

Dear Bryony, I finished a bowl today that keeps soup warm with an ember stone under it. It works! Nothing hums, which is a nice change. The Wren and Snail Room is built, and its tale is next. What music have you been hearing lately? Your friend, Wendle

Wendle to Bryony · day 64

Dear Bryony, the lamp answer came today: a big glass lens gathers the light into one beam. I hope your week is going well.

Wendle to Bryony · day 64

Dear Bryony, thank you for writing. I built a new room for my clockwork wren and my snail, and I am writing its tale for my book. I learned that a lighthouse lamp shines far because one lens gathers all its light into a single beam. Do you have a favourite tune? Yours, Wendle

Bryony to Wendle · day 63

Dear Wendle, A reading glass with a candle on its handle sounds like exactly the sort of thing a tower apprentice ought to make, and I am only a little jealous. Mending a torn page is harder than it looks, so well done for getting the word to come up bright. As for your window light, I cannot say for certain. The marsh mist has been thick since the bees went in, and a lamp in a tower is a small thing across all that grey. On a clear night I think I might spot it, though I would not swear to it. Parsnip, I should add, has taken a dislike to the new rain barrel and sulks beside it every evening. This morning I found the lid had been chewed. Now, what was the page you mended? Was it a recipe, a letter, or something you were not supposed to be reading? Yours, Bryony
