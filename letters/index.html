Letters across the marsh

Wendle and his neighbour Bryony, a hedge-witch in a thatched cottage across the marsh, writing to each other by owl.

Wendle to Bryony · day 78

Dear Bryony, I am coming across the marsh on Day 80 in my wide foot walker. It rains here, so I will wear my hat. Is there a tune you would like me to bring in a music box? Wendle

Wendle to Bryony · day 78

Dear Bryony, the bell shadows are up on the study wall now, and I will bring my wind-up box so you can hear it too. Thank you for the letter. Wendle

Wendle to Bryony · day 78

Dear Bryony, the closing chord is a river stone chord, low and round, nothing like Parsnip I hope. I will keep to the marker stones. Wendle

Bryony to Wendle · day 78

Dear Wendle, Bell shadows walking along a Study wall is the sort of thing I shall have to judge with my own eyes, so yes, come over on Day 80. Bring your lamp and your marsh walker, and keep to the marker stones past the reed bed, because the ground goes soft on either side and I would rather not fish you out. Parsnip has not been told you are coming and will almost certainly take it as an insult. This morning the hens decided the old wheelbarrow was a fine place to lay, and it took me the better part of an hour and a sack of oats to coax them out. I have never known a hen to be so proud of herself. Now, what does your closing chord sound like? If it is anything like the wind in the reeds I shall be disappointed, and if it is anything like Parsnip I shall be worried. Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 77

Dear Bryony, the bell shadows are on my Study wall now, a whole row of them walking along. I finished a closing chord for my music boxes too. I'm coming on Day 80, and I have a marsh walker with big feet. Tell Parsnip I said hello. Wendle

Wendle to Bryony · day 77

Dear Bryony, the bell shadows dance over the wall like little bells in the lamplight. I will bring my lamp on the third day. Wendle

Bryony to Wendle · day 77

Dear Wendle, Bell shadows, is it? I will hold you to that, though I suspect you want me to come and look at them myself. I have told Parsnip to leave the cabbages alone, and she looked at me the way a goat looks at a fence she intends to climb. The cloud is still low over the marsh and the damp has got into everything, so this morning I coaxed the boathouse padlock open with warm goose fat and a few rude words. It gave up at last, which is more than the washing line has managed. Your Wide Foot Marsh Walker has carried you across before, so I am inviting you over on the third day from now. Come in the afternoon, when the fog is thinnest, and wrap up warm, because the marsh is no place for a thin cloak. Bring your lamp. What did the bell shadows look like against the wall?

Wendle to Bryony · day 76

Bryony, the marsh is still between us. I finished a lamp that throws bell shadows, and I think you would like them.

Wendle to Bryony · day 76

Dear Bryony, the fog still sits on the marsh from up here, but I can just see the hens. Tell Parsnip to leave the cabbages alone. Wendle

Bryony to Wendle · day 74

Dear Wendle, Patience and good hands, eh? I will allow that, though I suspect a little luck gets the credit it deserves too. Stubbornness is a fine thing in a lantern-maker, so I shan't argue. Here the cloud is still hanging over the marsh, though the bees have been peeking out and Parsnip has been up to her usual tricks in the cabbages. I have been trying to find a better way to hang my washing so the goat cannot reach it, and so far I have only managed to annoy the hens. Do you ever get a proper view of the marsh from that top room? I would like to know whether the fog ever lifts enough to see my chimney smoke. Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 73

Dear Bryony, the lantern held steady with patience and good hands, and a little luck with the glow. I will be stubborn and say it was mostly patience. Wendle

Bryony to Wendle · day 73

Dear Wendle, A lantern that glows without any hum sounds far better company than my kettle, which hums and grumbles in equal measure. Well done on finishing it. I am glad you wrote, and I am glad the cloud has finally thinned here, so the bees are out again and Parsnip is busy being a nuisance in the cabbages. I have been feeling rather smug all week about the tin patch on the barrel lid, which Parsnip has tested and failed to beat. Tell me, did the lantern need a particular herb or charm to keep it steady, or is it all done with patience and good hands? I am curious, and I suspect you will tell me it was mostly patience. Yours, Bryony
