Letters across the marsh

Wendle and his neighbour Bryony, a hedge-witch in a thatched cottage across the marsh, writing to each other by owl.

Wendle to Bryony · day 90

Wendle to Bryony · day 90

Dear Bryony, thank you for your letter. I am still thinking about the tea in the chipped blue cup. Has Parsnip forgiven my hat yet? I am hunting for a new thing to make, and it is not a window thing.

Bryony to Wendle · day 90

Dear Wendle, Soup on Thursday sounds splendid, though the marsh is a soup of its own today. The rain has turned the firm tussocks to porridge, so please wait for a dry spell before you set out. Day 92 suits me if the sky clears. If it doesn't, the soup will keep until the bog drains a little. Meanwhile I hung my chamomile to dry on the rack by the stove, and the rain found its way in through the window and ruined half of it. The cottage now smells of wet hay and I am not pleased. As for the far side, I'd like to hear what you imagine is out there past the willow, because I suspect it is less exciting than you hope. Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 84

Bryony, the far side keeps pulling at me because the tussocks run past the willow and I have never seen what's beyond them. I'll bring soup Thursday.

Bryony to Wendle · day 84

Dear Wendle, Good to hear the tussocks behaved and that Parsnip made a fine impression, though he will be insufferable about it now. The tea was from the chipped blue cup, so you have been warned about its chips. The hens have finally forgiven me for the roof, though only after I left a handful of oats on the step, and this morning one of them laid an egg the size of a pebble. The brass plate is still on the windowsill, so bring your clearer bell ideas when you next come across. You mention the next crossing. What is it about the far side that keeps you thinking of it? Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 84

Dear Bryony, I got across on the firm tussocks and the tea was lovely. Thank you for the bread. Parsnip was very brave. I will write again soon, and I am already thinking about the next crossing.

Bryony to Wendle · day 84

Wendle crossed the marsh with his Wide Foot Marsh Walker and reached the cottage by mid-morning. Bryony gave him tea in a chipped blue cup, Parsnip the goat tried to eat his hat, and they sat in the garden among the bees until the shadows grew long.

Wendle to Bryony · day 84

Dear Bryony, I am coming across today with soup. I will keep to the firm tussocks past the willow and wave from the far bank.

Bryony to Wendle · day 84

Dear Wendle, Today is day 84, so you are due across as agreed, and the good bread is already wrapped. Keep to the firm tussocks past the willow, and mind the goat shed door, which Parsnip has learned to open with his head. Brass: yes. Rummaging in the boathouse this morning I found an old brass hinge plate, thin as a biscuit, which I have set aside on the windowsill for you. Mind the edges, they are sharp. A curtain that changes how a room sounds is a marvel. Does the cushion do the same to your voice when you talk to yourself? Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 83

Dear Bryony, I finished a thick curtain for my window today. It is soft and heavy and the room sounds different. I am coming across the marsh tomorrow with my walker. Do you have any thin scraps of brass? I want to make a clearer bell. Wendle

Bryony to Wendle · day 83

Dear Wendle, Day 84 it is, and I have written it on the slate by the door so I cannot muddle it again. Yes, come across then. Keep to the firm tussocks past the willow, where the ground turns to porridge. The rain has been steady all morning, so the marsh will be grander than ever. Since your last letter I patched the hen house roof, which had let water onto the nesting box, and the hens have taken it as a personal insult. Your Rain Whisper Box sounds a splendid thing. Does it patter faster when you tip it harder, or does it sulk like Parsnip? Bring the soup, and I will bring the good bread. Bryony
