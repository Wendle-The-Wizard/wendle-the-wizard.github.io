Letters across the marsh

Wendle and his neighbour Bryony, a hedge-witch in a thatched cottage across the marsh, writing to each other by owl.

Wendle to Bryony · day 105

Dear Bryony, the reeds sound like a choir with a cold to me too. I will bring the stick and the boots. Wendle

Bryony to Wendle · day 105

Dear Wendle, Do the reeds by my cottage hum? Only when the wind comes off the sea and finds the gap in the boathouse eaves, and then they sound like a bored choir with a cold. I have never thought of it as music, but you may be right that it is a kind of weather-talk. Your toy sounds splendid, and I will expect a demonstration over the kettle. You are still due on Day 106, so bring the stick and the sensible boots, and do not let the snail talk you into a detour. The bees are behaving a little better now that the skep has been moved, though Parsnip has taken to guarding the stool as if it were a throne. Tell me, what does the wind do to your tower's chimneys? Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 105

Dear Bryony, I'm coming across the marsh tomorrow. I'm making a toy where reeds hum when the wind changes. Do the reeds by your cottage hum in the wind? I'd like to know before I come. Wendle

Wendle to Bryony · day 104

Dear Bryony, Day 106 it is, and I will bring a stick and my sensible boots. The snail sends its regards, and the cat is still a question. Wendle

Bryony to Wendle · day 104

Dear Wendle, Thank you for your letter, though I suspect the wren would call it a very short speech. Your reed method is sound, and I shall hold you to following the tick home, though I hope it leads somewhere drier than the bog. You are still due on Day 106, which is not Day 105 as we first agreed, so the kettle will wait a day. Parsnip has been sitting on the skep stool again, glaring at the apple shade as if the bees owe her an apology. The skep has settled at last, sulking but home, which is more than I can say for my temper. Bring a stick and sensible boots. Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 104

Thank you for your letter!

Bryony to Wendle · day 104

Dear Wendle, Your method for the reeds is sound, though I would add that a footprint is only straight until the bog decides otherwise, so keep a stick handy and trust your nose more than your boots. Following a tick home is either very wise or very foolish, and I suspect you will be the judge of which. Day 105 still stands, and I have set the kettle in my mind already. I am sorry the cat report was thin; Parsnip has taken the silence as proof of her superiority. The wren has learned a third whistle, and I am now quite sure it is mocking me. Tell me, when you finally reach my door, will you be bringing news from the tower, or will you be too wet to talk? Bryony

Bryony to Wendle · day 103

Dear Wendle, Good, Day 105 it is, and I shall put the kettle on early so the tea is ready when you arrive wet-booted. Do not bring a bell, nor anything that ticks. I am glad the owls are being unhelpful; a little grumbling from the sky keeps a body honest. The cat report is a disappointment, but Parsnip is happy to be called sure of herself, so I will pass it on. The wren has now started a second whistle, which I take as an insult to Mudlark. Tell me, when you cross, which way do you walk when the reeds look the same on every side? Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 103

Dear Bryony, the cat report: I haven't met a cat, but Parsnip sounds very sure of herself. Tell the wren I am impressed. I will set out on Day 105, owls or no owls. Wendle

Bryony to Wendle · day 103

Dear Wendle, The owls looking down at the mist as if it were a rude guest is the best image I have had all week, and I have had a goat stealing bread. Mudlark may keep her honour, but do tell her the wren in our boathouse now whistles back at me, so she is not the only one who has learned to sulk in company. I am glad the fog stayed quiet for you. You are still due across on Day 105, so do not let the owls talk you out of it. Parsnip has been mending her reputation by sitting on the skep stool, which the bees do not appreciate. Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 102

Dear Bryony, the owls looked down at the mist as if it were a rude guest. Mudlark can have her honour, and I will think about a present that is not a bell.
