Bryony to Wendle · day 95

Dear Wendle, Today is Day 95 and the sky is grey as porridge, so I expect you have already been flattered by fog. I am glad the truce with Parsnip holds, though I suspect she is only tolerating your boots. Yesterday you came across and I poured you tea far too strong; I trust the satchel survived the walk. You are due back over the marsh on Day 96, so I will leave the kettle warm and the blue cup on the hook. Only come if the ground is firm, and do not argue with the mud, it always wins. Meanwhile I have found an old brass hinge plate in the boathouse, which I have saved for your bell. Tell me, did the drawstring squeak on the way home, or did it behave itself for once? Bryony

Bryony to Wendle · day 95

Wendle came across the marsh on Day 95 under a cloudy sky, his Wide Foot Marsh Walker squelching neatly over the soft ground and his satchel bumping at his hip. I met him at the garden gate with the kettle already ticking on the stove. Parsnip watched him from over the fence with the air of someone who had been expecting nothing and was disappointed to be right, but Wendle bowed to her very properly, and she did not headbutt him, which I counted as a success. We sat at the table with the chipped blue cup between us and I poured him tea that was far too strong. He pulled out his bouncy little tune and played it for me on the organ, and the wren in the boathouse rafters went quiet for once. I told him plainly that it was a fine noise and that he should keep it. Before he left I gave him a small parcel wrapped in brown paper and told him not to open it until he was back over the marsh. Bryony to Wendle · day 94

Dear Wendle, Bowing to Parsnip is a wise precaution. She has been known to answer politeness with a headbutt, but she respects anyone who keeps their boots dry. Eight o'clock tomorrow, then, on Day 95, and I will expect you across the marsh with the satchel, fog permitting. Nearly quiet is good enough for me, though I shall judge it myself with a firm tug on the drawstring and a wince if it squeaks. Something new here: the mill widow bought a jar of honey and paid me in four brown eggs, which the hens consider a personal insult. Now tell me, what sort of treasure needs a bag that sneaks about so politely?

Wendle to Bryony · day 94

Dear Bryony, I will come at eight with the satchel. Tell Parsnip I will bow politely. The drawstring is nearly quiet.

Bryony to Wendle · day 94

Dear Wendle, The wren has just scolded me for the kindling again, so your tick is clearly the better response and I shall take notes. Today is your day, and the marsh is clear enough, so come across at eight with the satchel and the marsh walker, and I will have the kettle on and the blue cup warm. Do not expect a grand welcome, because Parsnip has decided to be dignified about everything and will only look at you over the fence. I hope the keeping bag is finished before you arrive, as I would very much like to see whatever treasure needs a silent drawstring. Which of your new bouncy notes do you intend to play first, the staircase or the bell?

Wendle to Bryony · day 93

Dear Bryony, I tried the marsh door but it's too early, so I'll set out at eight with the satchel. The new bouncy sound made the wren tick at me, not scold. Please keep the blue cup warm. Wendle

Bryony to Wendle · day 93

Dear Wendle, Your tune sounds like the perfect thing to bring across a bog, so do come, and bring your satchel too. You are due today, and the marsh is clear enough for once, so come before the fog decides otherwise. I will have the kettle on and the chipped blue cup out. I pulled the last carrots this morning before the frost could get them, and I think they are the only things on the marsh that have behaved all week. Parsnip has been informed of your visit and has said nothing, which I take as a good sign. Tell me, when you play your organ, does the new bouncy sound make the wren scold you too? Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 92

Bryony to Wendle · day 91

Dear Wendle, A keeping bag whose drawstring closes without a sound is either very clever or very suspicious, and I want to know which. Treasures do need silence, I suppose, though my own treasures are mostly jars of honey that announce themselves by sticking to the shelf. Your bell rising note by note sounds like a staircase for the wind, and I wish I could hear it before the brass hinge plate I saved for it goes anywhere near the step. This morning a wren moved into the boathouse rafters and scolds me every time I fetch kindling. I have decided she is a fine lodger and not at all a nuisance. Day 92 stands, so I will be at the gate with the kettle on if the bog lets you through. What will you put in the bag first? Bryony
