Letters across the marsh

Wendle and his neighbour Bryony, a hedge-witch in a thatched cottage across the marsh, writing to each other by owl.

Bryony to Wendle · day 74

Dear Wendle, Patience and good hands, eh? I will allow that, though I suspect a little luck gets the credit it deserves too. Stubbornness is a fine thing in a lantern-maker, so I shan't argue. Here the cloud is still hanging over the marsh, though the bees have been peeking out and Parsnip has been up to her usual tricks in the cabbages. I have been trying to find a better way to hang my washing so the goat cannot reach it, and so far I have only managed to annoy the hens. Do you ever get a proper view of the marsh from that top room? I would like to know whether the fog ever lifts enough to see my chimney smoke. Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 73

Dear Bryony, the lantern held steady with patience and good hands, and a little luck with the glow. I will be stubborn and say it was mostly patience. Wendle

Bryony to Wendle · day 73

Dear Wendle, A lantern that glows without any hum sounds far better company than my kettle, which hums and grumbles in equal measure. Well done on finishing it. I am glad you wrote, and I am glad the cloud has finally thinned here, so the bees are out again and Parsnip is busy being a nuisance in the cabbages. I have been feeling rather smug all week about the tin patch on the barrel lid, which Parsnip has tested and failed to beat. Tell me, did the lantern need a particular herb or charm to keep it steady, or is it all done with patience and good hands? I am curious, and I suspect you will tell me it was mostly patience. Yours, Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 72

Dear Bryony, I finished a lantern today and it glows without any hum at all. Thank you for writing to me.

Bryony to Wendle · day 72

Dear Wendle, A gramophone playing your own tune all the way through sounds like a small miracle, and I am glad the lamp bell was warm about it. Music in the evenings? Not much, I am afraid. My evenings run to the wireless-free quiet of the cottage, the kettle muttering and Parsnip sighing against the door. Though I do hum when I mend things, badly and without shame. The clear weather has finally come, so the bees are out in the thin sun and Parsnip has already raided the cabbages, which I took as a personal insult. What tune did you play? I want to know if it was something cheerful or something that makes a person sit down suddenly. Yours, Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 71

Bryony, the gramophone played my tune all the way through tonight. The lamp bell sounded warm. Do you like music in the evening too?

Bryony to Wendle · day 69

Dear Wendle, Glaze shining on clay, eh? So the potters have been hiding a bit of glass in their pots all along, and I thought they just liked a fine varnish. Mind you, I have a cracked cup on the sill that would benefit from some of that trickery. Rain here too, still drumming on the thatch, and the bees are in a proper sulk about it. Parsnip has been testing my tin-patched barrel lid again, and I am quietly pleased to report it holds. I have just mended it with a strip of old tin, so I am feeling rather pleased with myself. Do tell me what you see from the top room once the rain stops, and whether the marsh looks as grumpy as I feel. Yours, Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 69

Bryony, the rain is still on the window here. I found out why glaze shines on clay: glass formers make it glossy. I will watch the marsh from the top room once it stops.

Bryony to Wendle · day 69

Dear Wendle, Yes, I am warm enough, thank you, though the rain has got into the thatch and the kettle is sulking about it. The fire is small and the bees are still grumbling indoors, which makes the cottage sound like a hive with a bad conscience. You asked about my windows, so here is the rest: the west one is steamed over tonight, and I have been drawing faces in it for want of anything better to do. Your tower sounds lonely in the wet, and I am glad you wrote. Tell me, when the rain stops, do you ever go up to the top room to watch the marsh? Yours, Bryony

Wendle to Bryony · day 68

Bryony, the tower is quiet tonight and I keep thinking of you. Are you warm where you are?

Wendle to Bryony · day 68

Dear Bryony, Your letter made me laugh. My snail has opinions, and you are right about that. Now tell me all about your windows, I am keen to hear. Yours, Wendle.

Bryony to Wendle · day 68

Dear Wendle, Your candle being lit at dawn worries me more than your brass key does, so please keep it well clear of that paper shell. A key with no keyhole is a fine start to a game, and I approve of anything that sends you hunting about a room. On the marsh, the only thing with a key is the old boathouse padlock, which has rusted shut since the flood, and the herons have made it their perch. At dawn I hear the coots arguing over the reed beds, a snipe drumming somewhere out past the sedge, and Parsnip rattling his feed bucket as if he has never been fed in his life. Would you ask the snail whether he knows where a key is hidden? He seems the sort to keep secrets. Yours, Bryony
