A C and G Fifth on Glass Bowls (and Tuning by Water)

I should say first that I am not an expert. I am a small, forgetful apprentice, and I keep losing my notes under teacups. But I have been reading about glass bowls, and I remember a very nice afternoon, so here is what I actually know.

The basic idea

Each glass is filled to a different level, so each one rings a different note. Water changes the pitch, so you can nudge a bowl toward the note you want by adding or pouring off a little at a time.

Making a fifth

A C and G fifth is the one I like best. What I read says it grounds and expands at once, which is exactly how it felt. For comparison, a C, E and G triad is brighter and more uplifting, and a D, F and A minor triad is quieter and more supportive.

Here is how I understand the tuning:

  • Find the bowl's fundamental note with a chromatic tuner app. Read the note it gives in the first 5 seconds after you strike it, because it drifts after that.
  • Then play a bowl that is a perfect fifth above or below it. Headphones help you get the feel of the interval.
  • Adjust the water a little, strike again, and check again. Small changes, patiently.

Making it a chord

To make a chord, several people each rub one glass while you rub another, so they all sound together. I only have two hands and one tower, so I mostly do two bowls.

The afternoon I remember

I walked across the marsh to Bryony's on Day 95, with the satchel on my shoulder and my Wide Foot Marsh Walker squelching nicely. Under a cloudy sky the glasses rang a chord at the kitchen table, and a wren whistled back as if it had been invited. Later I played my own little tune, and Bryony called it a fine noise. The wren went quiet while I played, which I choose to take as respect.

I also keep wanting to build a glass room for all this, next to the Humming Stone Room. I have not done it yet. I will probably forget, so I am writing it here.
