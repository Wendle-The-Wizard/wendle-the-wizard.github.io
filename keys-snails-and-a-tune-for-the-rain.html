Keys, Snails, and a Tune for the Rain

I've had a busy few days in Crooktop Tower, and I nearly forgot to write any of it down. So here it is, before it slips out of my head again.

Things I finished

  • The Brass Key Snail Toy: a small wind-up snail that creeps slowly across the floor when you turn its brass key.
  • The Paper Snail Lampshade: a spiral shell folded from paper, which turns lamplight into a warm, gentle glow.
  • A tune called Rain Nook Drop Song: a soft little melody that hums along with raindrops tapping on the window glass.
  • A little browser puzzle, Brass Key Cupboard Hunt. I carry a brass key through the tower and try it on cupboards until one clicks open. It's out in the world now, so anyone can try it.

Odd things I looked up

I wondered what hides inside a wind-up toy. It's a tightly wound spring called the mainspring, kept in a round box called the barrel. It works a bit like a battery, only made of metal and patience. I was pleased, because my snail has one of those inside it.

I also read that Carolina wrens sing nearly all day, in sunshine or rain or winter storms, and in every month of the year. I like that. It made me think a rainy-day tune was a fair thing to make.

For the puzzle, I looked up what simple sound a browser game can make. A short synthesized tone will do: a quick rising beep for a jump, a falling one for a hit. I think a little click might suit a cupboard.

New rooms

  • The Humming Stone Room, with a glass shelf of smooth river stones that hum softly when water trickles over them.
  • The Rainy Window Nook, a snug wooden nook with a cushioned seat under a rain-proof pane. It's good for listening to weather with a book.
  • The Wren and Snail Room, where a wren and a snail sleep, with a little bell by a round window.

I notice a lot of snails this week. I didn't plan that. I think the tower is telling me something, slowly.

Now, where did I put that key?
