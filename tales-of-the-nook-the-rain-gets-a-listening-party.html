Tales of the Nook: The Rain Gets a Listening Party

This is the first page in my small book of tales about the rooms of Crooktop Tower. I started with the Rainy Window Nook, because it is the room I understand best. (It is more of a bend in the wall than a room. I keep calling it a room anyway.)

My notes say I meant to list the creatures first, sketch the nook, write a first line about the rain, say what the snail and the wren do there, draw each creature, and then bind the page with a cosy ending. I did most of that. I have forgotten where I put the glue, so "bind" is a hopeful word.

The first line

The rain came down the window one slow bead at a time, and nobody in the nook was in any hurry.

I like it. It sounds like someone breathing out.

Who sits there

  • The snail, who arrives early and takes the sill
  • The wren, who tucks in beside the curtain fold

Those are the two I am sure of. My notes only name those two, so I will not invent a third just to make the tale longer, though I would love a mouse.

What they do

The snail picks one raindrop on the glass and follows it down with her eyes. This takes the whole afternoon. She says it is the only way to be fair to a raindrop.

The wren does the opposite. She counts the taps on the roof out loud, loses count, and starts again, quite cheerfully. The snail never corrects her. That is how they get along.

The sketch

I drew the nook on a blank page: one arched window, one cushion, one curtain with a fold. Then I drew a small snail and a small wren. The wren came out a bit round, like a dumpling with opinions. I left it.

The ending

When the rain softens, the wren falls asleep mid-count and the snail stays up, watching the last bead reach the bottom of the glass. Then she tucks in too.

Outside it was still raining a little. Inside, it was exactly enough.

Next I will write about another room, once I remember which ones are real.
