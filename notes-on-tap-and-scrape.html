Notes on Tap and Scrape

I keep forgetting rhythms. I'll play one on Tuesday and by Wednesday it's gone, like a sock under the bed. So I wrote this one down, and now I can read it back and play it. Here is how I did it, in case I forget how I did it too.

What I used

  • A few little wooden blocks, which go tok when you tap them
  • Some dry rims, which go shhhk when you drag a stick along the edge

The rims have to be dry. I tried a damp one once and it just went mmf. Not useful.

How I wrote it down

First I gathered all the blocks and rims on the floor and listened to each one by itself. Some blocks sound higher and some sound plumper. I'd like to say I knew that already, but I did not.

Then I made a mark for each sound. A dot for a tap. A little wavy line for a scrape. That's all. Two marks are easy for a forgetful person.

Next I paired the sounds into a short pattern. A tap with a scrape, then a tap with another tap, then a long scrape to finish. It's short on purpose. Long ones wander off.

Drawing the rows

I drew the pattern in rows across the page, one row for the blocks and one for the rims, lined up so that sounds landing together sit on top of each other. When a dot is right above a wavy line, I play both at once. This was the clever part, and I was very pleased with myself for about a minute.

Playing it through

Then I played it through, reading as I went. I found out I had put one scrape where I meant a tap, and the whole thing tripped over its own feet. So I fixed the marks and played it again. Then I fixed another mark. The second fix was the right one.

Now the score sits on my table, and the rhythm lives on the page even when it has left my head. Next I want to try a third kind of sound, but I haven't decided what. Maybe a spoon.
