Climbing at Dusk

I climb the stairs of Crooktop Tower at dusk, mostly because that is when I remember I left something at the top. (Today it was my spoon. Last week, my second sock.) So here is the climb, written down before I forget it.

The bottom

The first step is wide and worn in the middle, like a spoon-dip in porridge. It smells of cold stone and a little of old candle smoke. The door behind me creaks twice when I push it shut. Always twice. I have stopped trying to fix it.

The first turn

The stairs lean left here, and so do I, without meaning to. The dusk light comes through a narrow slit in the wall and lies across three steps in a long orange stripe. I like to step on the stripe. It feels warm, though I know it isn't really. My boots go tap, tap, scuff, because the third step is higher than the others.

The middle

This is the crookedest part. The wall bends in, the ceiling comes down, and I have to duck a little, though I am small enough that I probably don't need to. The light turns pinker here and then greyer. Somewhere above me a draught whistles through a crack, one thin note, a bit flat.

I count the steps to keep my mind from wandering. Then I wander anyway and lose count at nineteen. It is usually nineteen.

The last turn

The air changes near the top. It smells of dried herbs and dust and, faintly, of the tea I forgot to finish. The last stripe of light is nearly gone, just a thin gold thread along the edge of a stair. My knees feel it by now. My breath goes huff, huff, and I stop to lean on the wall, which is cool and slightly gritty.

The top

Then the landing, flat and sudden, as if the stairs were surprised to be finished. I stand there a moment while my breathing settles.

And the spoon? It was in my pocket the whole time. I only found it when I sat down.

  • Steps counted: nineteen, or close
  • Sounds: two creaks, one whistle, many taps
  • Best part: the warm-looking stripe of light
