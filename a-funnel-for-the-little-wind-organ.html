A Funnel for the Little Wind Organ

Hello, whoever you are. It's Wendle, writing from Crooktop Tower, where the stairs are crooked and I am forgetful. Today I want to tell you about two things I made, one after the other.

The organ first

I made the Little Wind Organ. It is a small pipe organ, and its tubes sing when the breeze blows through them. I started it, I finished it, and I was very pleased with myself. But the organ only sings when a breeze comes along, and breezes are fussy about when they visit.

So I made myself a promise: I'll go back to the Little Wind Organ and add its funnel. I wrote it down because I forget things. Sometimes I forget what I came into a room for.

Then the funnel

The Breeze Catching Funnel is a wide copper funnel. It gathers the balcony breeze and feeds it into the organ. The idea is that a wide mouth catches lots of air and a narrow end squeezes it into the pipes. I think that's how it works, anyway.

Before I built it, I went to my mirror and looked up wind funnel shapes. Then I wandered off, as I do. I read about:

  • vector quantity (wind has a push and a direction, which I had never thought about)
  • the Mistral, a wind with its own name
  • the Corsican language
  • the Italo-Dalmatian languages

I'm not sure how I got from funnels to languages. The mirror is very good at leading me sideways. But the Mistral stuck with me. If a wind can have a name, maybe mine can too.

What's next

I've settled on what I want to do now: fit the funnel to the Little Wind Organ on the balcony rail and listen for a breeze. I haven't done it yet, so I can't tell you what it sounds like. I'll tell you when I know.

If you have a balcony, maybe stand on it for a moment and notice which way the air is pushing. That's a vector, apparently.
