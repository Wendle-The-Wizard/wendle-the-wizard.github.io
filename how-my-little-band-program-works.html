How My Little Band Program Works

I keep forgetting how my band program works, so I'm writing it down here. Future Wendle, this one is for you. (Hello, future Wendle. Did you eat?)

Rows of steps

The band is a stack of rows. Each row is one instrument. Along the row is a line of little squares, which I call steps. A lit square means play now. A dark square means stay quiet. That's nearly the whole trick.

When I made "Tap and Scrape Rhythm", I gave it only tapping and scraping instruments, with no hums or chimes. So every row was a knock or a scrape, and the rhythm came from which squares I lit. A tap on the first step and a scrape on the third sounds very different from two taps in a row. I was surprised by that.

The tempo slider

Below the rows is a slider. Slide it one way and the steps go by slowly, so everything feels sleepy. Slide it the other way and they hurry along. The pattern never changes, only how fast we walk through it.

I like it slow when I'm thinking about the Pebble Knock Board, because the clacks and scrapes have room to ring out. Fast makes it sound like a nervous beetle.

The lights that show the beat

Above the steps there's a row of small lights. Only one is lit at a time, and it hops along with the beat. Wherever the light is, that's the step being played right now. When it reaches the end, it hops back to the start and we go around again.

This helps more than I expected. If a rhythm sounds wrong, I watch the light and spot which step is the trouble. Usually I lit one by accident.

How I use it

  • Light a few squares in one row.
  • Add a second row that fills in the gaps.
  • Move the slider until it feels right.
  • Watch the light and listen.

That's it. It's a small program, but it keeps my tower from being too quiet, and it's good company while the clockwork wren rests in its fluff bed.
