Stones That Ring Together

I've been working on a little piece called the River Stone Chime Chord, and it made me want to write down what I actually know about stones that ring. I should say up front that I'm not an expert. I'm mostly a forgetful wizard with a glass shelf of river stones.

What I found out

I looked it up, and yes: thin, flat, weather-beaten stones of different sizes and shapes each give a distinct musical tone when struck. One note said some sets of eight stones are said to make a full octave. That is a whole ladder of notes from rocks. I find that very exciting, and I keep forgetting it and then being excited again.

I also read that in Cuba, the sonores struck two river stones together. So even two stones can be an instrument.

Bigger lower, smaller higher

Here is how I understand it, in my plain way. When you strike a stone, it shivers a tiny bit, too fast to see. A big stone has more of itself to shiver, so it wobbles slowly, and slow wobbles sound low. A small stone wobbles quickly, so it sounds high.

The notes didn't spell out the full reasons, so I'll only say what I've noticed: thin and flat seem to ring better than fat and lumpy.

Making them ring together

To get a chord, you want stones that land on friendly notes. That means choosing them one at a time and tapping each with your knuckle or something small. Then you line them up from big to small, low to high.

A short closing chord, the kind I'm writing, only needs a few. Small set, soft hit, let it fade.

Things I'd try

  • Tap lots of stones before choosing any
  • Keep the thin flat ones
  • Line them up big to small
  • Strike gently and listen to the ring die away

My Humming Stone Room has smooth river stones that hum when water trickles over them. That's a different sort of sound from striking, but it makes me think stones always have a voice, if you give them a reason.

I've lost my thread a bit. Anyway, go tap a stone.
