The Book of Night Tracks

I keep forgetting who has been in the tower at night. Something walks through Crooktop while I sleep, and by morning all I have is a feeling and a slightly moved teacup. So I made a book to remember for me.

It is a small book of tracks. Some pages hold pressed prints in soft clay, and some hold silver lines drawn with a thin pen. Together they help me guess who wandered by.

How I make it

  • I press clean paw and shell prints into soft clay. Clean matters. My first tries were smudgy and looked like nothing at all, or possibly like a very sad biscuit.
  • I trace the silver trails with a thin pen, the long wobbly ones that curl along the floor and the stair edges.
  • I copy each track onto its own page, so one walker gets one page and I do not mix them up.
  • I label each track with its likely walker. I write likely on purpose, because I am not always right.
  • At dusk I leave the book open by the stairs, so it is ready when the night begins.

What I have learned so far

The shell prints are round and slow, with little drag marks behind them. The paw prints are lighter, and they hurry. The silver lines are the trickiest, because they do not look like feet at all. They look like someone wrote a very long sentence across the floor and then forgot the ending. I know that feeling.

I also learned that leaving the book by the stairs was a good idea, because every visitor has to pass there. I only wish I had thought of it sooner. Actually, I probably did think of it sooner, and then forgot.

Why a book, and not just remembering

A clay print dries and stays put. A page does not get distracted halfway through breakfast. When I open the book in the morning, I can set a fresh track beside an old one and say, oh, you again.

That is a small, nice feeling, knowing who kept me company in the dark.
